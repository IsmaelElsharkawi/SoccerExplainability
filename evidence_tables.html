All eight metrics, under the current definitions

S-AUC, T-AUC, T-AP and T-IoU (sweep) were redefined at commit ee51a38 as integrals of the IoU-vs-threshold curve. Tables 4 and 5 now carry them at the new definition, taken from the repeat runs on Drive; they are marked ●. Because they are IoU integrals rather than ranking scores, 0.5 is not chance and they land in the same low range as S-IoU and T-IoU — old S-AUC read 62–79, the new one reads 2–6. Tables 1–3 still show only the four single-cutoff metrics, whose per-tier and per-class breakdowns come from the sidecars.

Energy, Pointing, S-IoU and T-IoU are unaffected and current

These four are the single-cutoff metrics; the redefinition did not touch them. Every figure here was recomputed from the per-clip sidecars rather than transcribed, and reproduces the previously reported headline values exactly.

The intervals describe one thing only: how much the number would move if the benchmark had drawn a different 200 clips. They are not a significance test between models — overlapping intervals do not establish that two models are indistinguishable. Because all three were scored on the same 200 clips, a paired bootstrap on per-clip differences is the right instrument for any head-to-head claim, and it is far more sensitive than comparing these intervals by eye.

Read the clip SD column across models and the failure modes separate. MatchVision’s S-IoU swings about twice as much clip-to-clip (6.6) as SigLIP (3.4) or SoccerMaster (2.9): it grounds well on some clips and not at all on others, whereas SoccerMaster is uniformly low. Treat S-IoU’s narrow intervals with care — they are narrow because every S-IoU score sits near zero, which is a floor effect rather than precision.
